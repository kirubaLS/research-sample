01

Should you vectorise the NCERT book?

Yes — but never as a flat corpus, and vectors are the smaller half of the answer.

The short version: neither pure RAG nor a pure tree. Use the curriculum tree as a hard pre-filter, and vectors only inside the selected subtree. The NCERT book is not an unstructured document dump — it already has the exact hierarchy your product needs (Book → Chapter → Section → Worked Example → Exercise → Question). Throwing that structure away by chunking the PDF into 500-token blocks and running flat top-k is the single most common and most damaging RAG mistake in education products.

Maths-specific gotcha

Formulae destroy naive embeddings. Normalise LaTeX/MathML before embedding, keep a symbolic form alongside the text, and index the verbal statement of a formula as well as the notation — students and teachers search in words. A chunk that is 80% notation should carry a generated one-line natural-language gloss, or it will never be retrieved.

02

The retrieval architecture

One curriculum knowledge graph. Three retrieval paths that consume it very differently.

Why the prerequisite edges matter more than the embeddings

The dashed edges in Figure 2 carry the product’s most valuable inference. When a student fails composite-solids application questions, the actionable question is which gap it is: the composite reasoning itself, or the prerequisite surface-area formula for a cone. Because the Q-matrix already records mastery on the prerequisite sub-topics, the system can walk down the graph and answer that — and remediation targets the real gap rather than the symptom. No amount of vector search substitutes for this; it comes from the graph, hand-curated once per subject, and it is the second compounding asset after the Question Library.

Practical build order

For the pilot, the NCERT “vectorisation” you actually need is small: index the ~15 chapters’ exercise questions with their sub-topic and tier tags. That is a structured tagging exercise over roughly 400 items, not a document-AI project — and it powers the “suggested practice” section of every report. Passage-level embedding of the book’s prose can wait until v2, because nothing in the free pilot output requires it.

How the reinforcement layer actually learns

The reward is not a click. It is the change in measured mastery between two assessment cycles.

The honest constraint

At one school, two cycles and forty students you will have roughly 80 (student × recommendation) tuples. That is not enough to learn a policy — it is enough to validate the logging. Treat Stage 0 as instrumentation work whose payoff arrives at school ten, not school one. Any vendor claiming an RL-driven intervention engine on pilot-scale data is describing a rule engine.

From one school to a hundred

Direct answers, one line each

For the questions you asked, stripped of the diagrams.

Vectorise NCERT?Yes, but scoped. Tree filter first, vectors inside the subtree. Never flat top-k over the book.
RAG or tree?Both — the tree is the index, the vectors are the ranker. Figure 2.
What gets embedded first?The Question Library, not the textbook. Classification is the layer that pays for itself.
How does it “learn”?Four loops at four cadences (OCR, classification, psychometrics, policy). Only the fourth is RL, and it needs multi-school scale.
