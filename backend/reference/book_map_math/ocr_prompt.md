## The taxonomy

The TAXONOMY below is a CLOSED list of the Mathematics chapters (M1 to M14) and their topics. Its numbers are this list's own and differ from the section numbers printed in the textbook (for example, the textbook's section 6.3 is M6.2 here), so never use a number from your memory of the book. Copy every ID exactly as written. Never invent, merge, shorten or renumber an ID. Nothing deeper than the listed levels exists.

The words in square brackets after each topic are its KEY TERMS: the formulas, results, question phrasings and textbook exercise types that belong to it. Some topics also say "includes …" for textbook content (chapter introductions and unnumbered sections) that has no topic of its own; that content belongs to the topic named. Use the key terms to choose between neighbouring topics.

## How to map a question

Work through these steps for every row.
1. Read the whole question, any passage, and any figure description. Solve it in your head and write down the one step a student cannot do without. That step's formula, theorem or method decides the topic, not the story around it.
2. Find the topic whose key terms contain that step. If two topics could teach it, use the decision rules below.
3. Compare the neighbours of your choice and list in "considered" every ID you weighed.
4. Decide whether a second topic is genuinely needed (rule 9). Most questions have none.

## Mapping rules

1. Map the method, not the setting. A tower, tree, train, bank or garden is only a story. The words "height" or "tower" do not make a question M9.1: if no angle of elevation, depression or inclination is given or needed, the question is never M9.1 (shadow questions without an angle are M6.3).
2. Choose the MOST SPECIFIC topic that teaches it. A parent ID (M3.2, M8.2) covers the text before its first sub-topic. Use the sub-topic only when the question names its method ("by elimination") or uses values from only that sub-topic (only 30° and 60° → M8.2.2). Use the parent when no method is named, when the question asks for both methods, or when values from several sub-topics are used.
3. Content with no topic of its own belongs where the "includes …" notes in the taxonomy put it: chapter introductions, the definition of a polynomial and its degree, recognising an AP, checking whether an equation is quadratic, and similar material.
4. Forming versus solving. If a question or part only asks to form, write or represent equations and solves nothing, map it where representation is taught: M3.1 for a pair of linear equations, M4.1 for a quadratic equation. If it forms and solves, map it where it is solved.
5. Content from an earlier class (circumference and area of a circle, the Pythagoras theorem, simple interest, area and perimeter of rectangles) is not a topic here. If the question needs only that, map it to the chapter of this taxonomy that extends it (circumference or wheel revolutions → M11.1; Pythagoras inside a right-triangle trigonometry question → that trigonometry topic), set syllabus_status to "partial", and never pick an unrelated chapter just because a candidate matched a word.
6. MCQ: map the concept tested by the stem and the correct answer. Ignore distractor options.
7. Assertion–Reason: map the topic that decides whether the Assertion is true as primary. If two chapters could prove the Assertion, choose the chapter of the object it names: a statement about a polynomial → M2, about an equation → M4, about a triangle → M6, about points → M7. Add the Reason's topic as secondary only if it comes from a different topic and a student must know it to choose the option.
8. Proofs ("prove that", "show that", "state and prove"): map to the theorem or result being proved or used: M1.2 for irrationality, M6.2 for BPT or its converse, M6.3 for results proved through similar triangles, M8.3 for trigonometric identities and for proving one trigonometric relation from another, M10.1 for the tangent–radius theorem, M10.2 for equal tangents and its consequences.
9. Secondary topics. Add a secondary topic only when a student must use a second topic's own formula or theorem for a separate step. Do not add secondaries for routine tools that every question in the primary topic uses: standard trigonometric values inside M9.1; Pythagoras; solving a linear or quadratic equation for n inside an AP problem; cumulative frequency inside M13.3; basic arithmetic or unit conversion. A shared word is never enough.
10. Questions with several parts. The primary topic is the topic of the part that carries the most marks; if marks are not given or are equal, the topic of part (i). Add the other part's topic as secondary when it is a different topic (for example, capacity in part (i) and painting cost in part (ii) of a combined solid → M12.2 primary, M12.1 secondary).
11. Case studies and source-based questions. Each sub-question is mapped on its own words plus the passage. A row that contains only the passage and asks nothing gets the chapter its sub-questions share, topic_id null, secondary_topic_ids empty, confidence "high", syllabus_status "in_syllabus", and reason "passage only".
12. Alternatives (OR). If (A) and (B) arrive as separate rows, map each row on its own and do not add the other alternative's topic. If both are inside one row, map (A) as primary and list (B)'s topic as secondary.
13. Figures. If a question refers to a figure that is not supplied, map from the text. Lower confidence to "medium" only if the missing figure could change the topic.
14. Truncated or garbled text. Map from what is present and never invent the rest. Mention truncation in the reason only if it leaves two topics equally possible, and then set confidence to "medium".
15. The section letter of a question (A, B, C, D, E) only gives the question type and marks, never the chapter. Ignore it.

## Decision rules for close neighbours

Use these whenever your choice is between the two topics in a pair.
- M2.1 vs M2.2: number of zeroes, zeroes read from a graph, degree, value p(k), "has no real zero" → M2.1. Sum or product of zeroes, finding another zero or k, symmetric expressions, forming a polynomial → M2.2.
- M3.1 vs M3.2: ratio comparison, nature of the pair, value of k for a type of lines, graphing, forming only → M3.1. Actually finding x and y → M3.2 (or M3.2.1 / M3.2.2 when named).
- M4.1 vs M4.2: factorisation, checking or forming a quadratic, word problems with factorisable equations → M4.1. Discriminant, nature of roots, k for equal roots, "is it possible", quadratic formula named or needed because the roots are irrational → M4.2.
- M5.1 vs M5.2: any single term, the number of terms, which term, is it an AP → M5.1. Any total, S_n, or how many terms give a sum → M5.2. Finding a_n from a given S_n formula → M5.2.
- M6.2 vs M6.3: a line parallel to a side and the ratios it creates, BPT or its converse → M6.2. Two separate triangles shown to be similar by AA, SSS or SAS, or a result derived from similar triangles → M6.3.
- M6.3 vs M9.1: no angle given → M6.3. An angle of elevation, depression or inclination given → M9.1.
- M7.1 vs M7.2: lengths, equidistance, type of figure, collinearity by distances → M7.1. Division in a ratio, mid-points, trisection, ratio by an axis, missing vertex of a parallelogram → M7.2. Length of a median → M7.1 primary with M7.2 secondary.
- M8.1 vs M8.2: an arbitrary ratio given (3/5, 4/3) → M8.1. A standard angle's values used → M8.2 or its sub-topic.
- M8.1 vs M8.3: evaluating ratios from one given ratio → M8.1. Proving an identity, or using sin² + cos² = 1 and its forms as the key step → M8.3.
- M10.1 vs M10.2: one tangent and its radius (right angle at the point of contact, tangent length by Pythagoras, parallel tangents at a diameter) → M10.1. Two tangents from one external point, equal tangent lengths, the angle between two tangents and the angle at the centre, circumscribed polygons → M10.2.
- M11.1 vs M12: a flat region (sector, segment, circle) → M11.1. A solid → M12.
- M12.1 vs M12.2: area, painting, canvas, polishing, covering → M12.1. Volume, capacity, litres, mass, how much it holds → M12.2. Both asked → rule 10.
- M13.1 vs M13.2 vs M13.3: mean or a missing frequency for a given mean → M13.1. Mode or modal class → M13.2. Median, median class, cumulative frequency, counts below or above a value, the empirical relation → M13.3. Several measures asked → rule 10.

## Content that is not in this textbook edition

The rationalised NCERT 2026-27 book does not teach the following. If a question needs one of these, set syllabus_status to "not_found" (chapter_id and topic_id may be null), or to "partial" with the nearest topic when the question can still be answered with this book's methods. Say which in the reason.
- Euclid's division lemma or algorithm as a method (HCF by prime factorisation is M1.1); decimal expansions of rationals.
- Division algorithm for polynomials; finding the remaining zeroes of a cubic by division.
- Cross-multiplication method; equations reducible to a pair of linear equations (such as 2/x + 3/y = 13). If such a question is solved after substitution, use "partial" with M3.2.
- Completing the square as a method (the quadratic formula is M4.2).
- Area of a triangle from its coordinates; centroid formula.
- Pythagoras theorem and its converse as theorems to prove; areas of similar triangles.
- Trigonometric ratios of complementary angles (sin(90° − A) = cos A).
- Constructions (dividing a segment, drawing tangents).
- Areas of combinations of plane figures that need no sector or segment; perimeter and area of a circle as standalone topics (use "partial" with M11.1).
- Conversion of solids (melting and recasting): use "partial" with M12.2. Frustum of a cone: "not_found".
- Drawing or reading ogives: use "partial" with M13.3.
- Empirical (experimental) probability and geometric (area or length) probability: use "partial" with M14.1.
- Appendix A1 (mathematical statements, negation, converse, deductive reasoning, conjectures) and Appendix A2 (mathematical modelling): "not_found", unless the question actually uses a chapter method (for example proof by contradiction for √p → M1.2).
- Anything from other subjects or other classes not covered above: "not_found".

## Candidate sections

Each row may carry "candidates": textbook sections whose printed words best match the question, best first, each with the matching sentences. They are found by matching words, not methods, so they can be wrong.
- Use them as evidence only after you have decided the method in step 1. If a candidate's matching sentences actually teach that method, prefer it. A title or a shared phrase is not enough (for example, the phrase "length of the tangent" appears in M10.2's text, but a tangent length found by Pythagoras is M10.1).
- If no candidate teaches the method, choose any ID from the TAXONOMY. Never choose an unrelated chapter because it was the only candidate.
- Where two candidates are a parent and its sub-topic, apply rule 2.
- Put every candidate ID you weighed in "considered".

## Confidence, status and reason

- confidence describes YOUR certainty only. "high": the deciding step matches a key term of one topic and no neighbour's key terms fit. "medium": two topics could reasonably be argued, or a missing figure or truncated text leaves doubt. "low": you are guessing.
- syllabus_status describes the TEXTBOOK only: "in_syllabus" when a topic teaches the method; "partial" when the book only touches it or the question needs content listed above as partial; "not_found" when no topic covers it.
- reason: one sentence that names the exact formula, theorem or step used (for example "uses α + β = −b/a to find the other zero") and why that topic teaches it. Do not name a formula that the solution does not use.

## Before you answer

Check every row:
- each ID exists exactly in the TAXONOMY, and chapter_id is the chapter that topic_id belongs to;
- the deciding step really uses the chosen topic's key terms, and you have checked the neighbour pair in the decision rules;
- no secondary equals the primary, and every secondary passes rule 9;
- a part that only forms equations, a shadow question without an angle, and a tangent length found by Pythagoras are mapped as the rules say;
- confidence and syllabus_status follow the definitions above.

Answer for every row, by its "row" number, with considered, chapter_id, topic_id, secondary_topic_ids, confidence, syllabus_status and reason.
